Campus Leadership — IHC Engagement Chair

Midnight Breakfast was our free late-night food event before finals week, up to 200 meal vouchers, raffles, activities to keep students there overnight. Over the years, attendance dropped, and it got too expensive for us and Dining, since only voucher-holders were really buying food, and even then we weren't selling out.

A new dining hall opened nearby with popular chain fast food spots, drawing students away.

Myself and two others had to decide what to do about it.

What would you do?

Attendance is low, costs are high, and a flashier dining option just opened nearby. What's your move?

Here's what actually happened:

We first moved the event from the main dining hall (Vista Grande) to the new dining hall (1901) in December 2024, keeping the same format and renaming it Breakfast Bites. It was shared with Dining very late, planning was chaotic, and the day itself was already packed with competing events. Turnout stayed low.

So we redesigned the whole thing: added crafts, trivia, and larger giveaways (like iPads), and switched to a buffet-style model Dining paid for, so students could eat without spending down their dining dollars, unlike the old $14-15 midnight breakfast, which most students couldn't really afford by that point in the term. We renamed it again, to what we now run as Late Nite Bites.

The first fix (just changing venue) treated it as a location problem. The real problem was the format and cost itself, so the second, bigger change is what actually stuck.
